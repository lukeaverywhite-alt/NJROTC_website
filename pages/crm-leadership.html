Leadership is responsible influence

The manual describes leadership through example, knowledge, decisiveness, fairness, loyalty, initiative, judgment, enthusiasm, bearing, endurance, integrity, and concern for others. Effective cadet leaders accomplish the mission while helping the people entrusted to them learn and succeed.

Practical leadership principles

  • Know yourself and seek improvement.
  • Be technically and tactically proficient in assigned duties.
  • Know your people and look after their welfare.
  • Keep people informed and ensure tasks are understood and supervised.
  • Set the example, train as a team, and accept responsibility for decisions.

How the unit is organized

A cadet organization uses a chain of command to assign authority, pass information, and maintain accountability. Command and staff billets may include commanding and executive officers, senior enlisted leadership, department or staff officers, company leadership, platoon leadership, and squad leaders. Exact titles and staffing depend on the unit.

Use the current Bethel chain of command for assigned personnel. The CRM’s sample organization explains relationships but does not identify today’s officeholders.

Followership and delegation

Cadets lead and follow at different times. A good follower understands the task, asks useful questions, keeps the leader informed, and completes work without evading responsibility. A leader may delegate authority to perform a task but remains accountable for supervision and results.
