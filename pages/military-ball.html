More than a dance

Learning becomes a formal tradition

The Military Ball is one of the unit’s most formal annual experiences. Cadets prepare in class, welcome honored guests, share a formal meal, observe ceremonial traditions, and finish the evening with dancing.

Details and sequences can change, but the purpose remains constant: cadets practice confidence, courtesy, attention to detail, and respect in a setting where every part of their preparation matters.

Before the ball

Preparation starts in class

Cadets do not arrive unprepared. Classroom instruction turns an unfamiliar formal event into an experience they know how to navigate.

Dining & etiquette

A typical evening

From first arrival to final dance

The current event program always takes precedence, but a Bethel Military Ball commonly follows this flow.

  1. Arrival

    Guests are welcomed

    Cadets and guests arrive in formal attire, check in, and prepare to enter the dining room. Courtesy and military bearing set the tone immediately.

  2. Escort

    NS1 cadets guide the room

    Before the formal program, Naval Science 1 cadets may be assigned to escort attendees to their seats so guests feel welcomed and the room is ready.

  3. Ceremony

    The sword arch opens the evening

    When included in the approved program, the ceremonial sword arch frames the presentation or escort of distinguished guests. The guest of honor—often the evening’s invited speaker—receives formal recognition.

  4. Dinner

    Etiquette in action

    The formal meal gives cadets a chance to apply the dining etiquette and mannerisms practiced in class while sharing the evening with classmates, families, and guests.

  5. Tradition

    Seniors take the floor

    The senior class presents its carefully rehearsed, choreographed waltz—a signature moment that celebrates their preparation and time in the unit.

  6. Celebration

    The dance begins

    After the formal program and senior waltz, the floor opens for dancing and the unit celebrates together in a polished but social setting.

Dress standard

Arrive ready for inspection

Formal appearance is part of the event. The current unit instruction—not an archived web page—determines exactly what each attendee must wear.

SDB

Cadets in uniform

Male cadets have traditionally been required to wear the complete Service Dress Blue uniform. Every cadet assigned a uniform wears the prescribed combination with all authorized insignia, ribbons, grooming, and uniform regulations correctly observed.

  • Clean, pressed, and serviceable uniform
  • Correct placement of authorized items
  • Polished shoes and required accessories
  • Current grooming and appearance standards

FORMAL

Civilian formalwear

Attendees not directed to wear a uniform dress for a formal evening. Ladies have traditionally worn formal dresses or ball gowns; other guests typically choose comparably formal evening attire.

Current guest guidance may include additional expectations. Confirm attire with the unit before purchasing or altering clothing.
