Courtesy and respect

Military courtesy is respectful behavior grounded in tradition and discipline. Address seniors properly, respond promptly, avoid interrupting, and use “sir” or “ma’am” when appropriate. Courtesy applies in person, in formation, and during official communication.

The hand salute

The salute is an exchange of recognition between members of the military profession. When outdoors and in uniform, render or return salutes as instructed, face the person or colors, and use a correct, deliberate motion. Saluting rules vary with location, headgear, formation, and the honors being observed; practice the procedure demonstrated by unit instructors.

Colors, anthem, and honors

Show respect when the national anthem, “To the Colors,” or “Retreat” is sounded and when the national ensign passes. Stop, face the flag or music, and render the prescribed salute or civilian courtesy for the situation. Members of a formation follow the commands of the person in charge rather than acting independently.

Reporting and forms of address

Use a cadet’s grade and surname or the form directed by instructors. When reporting, approach with military bearing, halt at the proper distance, salute when required, identify yourself, state the purpose, and remain until dismissed. These habits create clear, respectful communication.
