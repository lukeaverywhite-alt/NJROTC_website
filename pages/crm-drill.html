Parts of a command

Most drill commands have a preparatory command, which identifies the movement, and a command of execution, which tells when to perform it. Command voice requires enough volume, clarity, cadence, and inflection for the formation to understand and respond together.

Positions and facing

Core terms include attention, parade rest, at ease, rest, present arms, order arms, right face, left face, and about face. Each position establishes the placement of the feet, hands, head, and body. Cadets should learn movements through instructor demonstration rather than text alone.

Formation vocabulary

Element
An individual, squad, section, platoon, company, or other unit that forms part of a larger unit.
File
A single column of cadets placed one behind another.
Rank
A single line of cadets placed side by side.
Interval
Space between individuals measured side to side.
Distance
Space between individuals or units measured front to rear.
Alignment
The arrangement of cadets on a straight line.
Cover
Aligning directly behind the cadet to the front.

Marching and unit movement

The section introduces steps, cadence, flanks, columns, obliques, marking time, halting, dressing, counting off, and forming or dismissing a unit. Timing and command details depend on the formation and movement. Follow instructor demonstrations and current governing drill guidance.

Use terminology in practice

  1. Identify the formation and your position in it.
  2. Listen for the preparatory command.
  3. Act together on the command of execution.
  4. Maintain interval, distance, alignment, cadence, and military bearing.
  5. Correct errors only as directed so the formation remains controlled.
